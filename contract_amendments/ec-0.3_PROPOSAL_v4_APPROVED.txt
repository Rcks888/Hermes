0. Response to review 1
-----------------------
F1  calendar-1 classified a missing run from its far end and from M15
    absence, so the label of an instant could depend on later data.
    ACCEPTED. calendar-1 is withdrawn. calendar-2 is a declared schedule: a
    function of the clock only, written from published exchange rules plus one
    dated broker change. The export verifies it and never defines it. Whether
    a minute is scheduled is therefore known at that minute. A control shows
    calendar-1 cannot classify an instant inside a run until the run ends.
    -> A3, C1.8, test 26.

F2  Joint absence of M1 and M15 does not prove a closure; "the broker
    published no prices" overstated the evidence.
    ACCEPTED. The sentence is withdrawn. Under calendar-2 any absence in
    scheduled time is a hole, whatever M15 shows, and an absent M15 bar
    there is C5.8(a), PATH_UNKNOWN. A control shows a 30-minute feed outage
    with both series absent is a hole under calendar-2 and a closure under
    calendar-1. The cost is stated in A3: 148 M15 slots absent in scheduled
    time [v3, was 139], against 11 under calendar-1.
    -> A3, test 26.

F3  The offset exception handled the session of D only.
    ACCEPTED. The offset enters C1.7 (Mon-Fri UTC), C2.2 (session of D), the
    session used for the spread distribution (C3.2), the UTC fields of the
    record (section 7) and, new in this amendment, the calendar itself. The
    exception no longer compares quantities. Every opportunity in the window
    is excluded, and inside the window a minute is scheduled if it is
    scheduled under either offset. So an uncertain offset can never turn a
    gap into a closure.
    -> A2, C1.3a, test 5.

F4  The quote-stop E0 exemption was self-contradictory and weakened E0.
    ACCEPTED. Withdrawn. E0 eligibility is as in ec-0.2. A frozen-tick cycle
    with execution fields is E0-eligible and must reproduce them from its
    logged inputs.
    -> A5.

F5  C4.6 (stop_consumed) changes which trades enter population C. It is a
    policy change outside the frozen rule-change menu, and the threshold was
    informed by observed live setups. The rationale also had two errors: the
    ratio depends on the spread model, and a volume cap does not break R
    accounting.
    ACCEPTED, all three parts. C4.6 is withdrawn and the baseline is
    unchanged. H6 stays open as a declared hazard with a report-only
    diagnostic that enters no gate. Corrections:
      (a) the executable stop distance is measured from the executable entry.
          For LONG that is the ask, which in replay is the bid plus the spread
          draw (C3.3), so any rule on it depends on the spread model;
      (b) a volume cap keeps R accounting intact, because C4.3 already scales
          cash by each trade's planned stop-loss cash. What it changes is the
          0g-5 sizing policy, which is the same category of change as a filter.
    Any policy for near-stop fills goes through pr-0.4's own change process,
    not through the contract.
    -> A6, test 28.

6. A6 - hazard H6, near-stop executable fills (AD6 re-asked)
-----------------------------------------------------------
Fact (signal 4). The executable stop distance was 0.70 against an original
6.51. The baseline sized 14.28 lots, RR 26.9. Under ec-0.2 such a trade
enters population C and its stop fills at the level (C3.8). For small
executable stops, C3.8's zero stop slippage is materially optimistic. That is
a known limitation of the cost model, not something this amendment fixes.

Proposed: no change to eligibility, sizing or costs. C4.6 is withdrawn.
  H6    stays OPEN in section 12, worded as above.
  R1 (report-only diagnostic, new in section 7):
        q = executable_sl_distance / |signal_close - original_sl|, per trade
        in population C. Every SIMULATION report shows the count and the
        share of total R for q < 0.25 and for q < 0.5, and the largest
        |R| trade. The cut points are descriptive only. They are never gates
        or thresholds, never enter a verdict, and nothing is fitted from them.
  Test 28 (new). Signal 4's logged quote gives q = 0.11 and stays
        `executable` with 14.28 lots. Signal 5 gives q = 1.27 with 0.68 lots.
        Both enter population C. Broken variant: any q filter, which drops
        signal 4 and must fail.
  Route for a policy change. A near-stop rule (filter, volume cap or slippage
        model) changes the 0g-5 baseline. It goes through PREREGISTRATION.md's
        change process, never through the contract. If the reviewer judges
        that even R1, being descriptive, needs a pr-0.4 version, R1 is dropped
        rather than versioned.
